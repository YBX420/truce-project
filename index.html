Abstract

Dynamic urban navigation requires useful detours, recovery from blocked motion, and a feasible continuation. We present TRUCE, a quadrotor navigation architecture coupling persistent route guidance, motion recovery, and predictive continuation checks. It evaluates native proposals and recovery motions against obstacle predictions and modeled execution, then feeds progress and rejection back to route selection. On 100 shared urban tasks with quadrotor dynamics, Truce-EGO reaches 83 goals versus 22 for official ROS EGO and 26 for EGO with Gatekeeper, with zero recorded contacts and 17 non-arrivals. Separately, we evaluate EGO, MINCO, and SANDO with and without TRUCE on the same 30 easy MetaUrban tasks in fake sim. Across the three planners, TRUCE increases arrivals from 77/90 to 88/90 and reduces contacts from five to zero. The results show improved task completion in the dynamics study and applicability across trajectory planners in fake sim; they do not establish unconditional physical safety.

Route-Guided Recovery and Continuation

Persistent route guidance

A maintained route retains its branch and cursor and supplies a temporary target. Progress follows the remaining route cost rather than only straight-line distance to the goal. New occupancy, predicted conflicts and repeated fallback can revise the route. A route that ends at a reachable frontier remains partial guidance, not a complete mission solution.

Motion selection and recovery

TRUCE preserves usable native motion before expanding recovery search to route-following segments, lateral detours, moving escape and braking. Each candidate starts from the current state and must pass motion acceptance. Moving recovery matters when residual vehicle motion or an approaching actor makes waiting unsafe.

Predictive continuation and renewal

Acceptance checks the timed reference and modeled execution, then tests a subsequent response from the predicted switch state. The EGO dynamics configuration uses a 0.8 s moving preview and admits waiting over the next 0.1 s update interval. New observations renew the checks. Exhausted search reports an unavailable safe action; finite previews do not establish indefinite safety.

Rejoining the route across planners

Adapters preserve EGO B-splines, MINCO quintics and SANDO time-stamped polynomial segments. Replanning uses measured position, velocity and acceleration. The route cursor advances from executed position, and checked re-entry returns control to native motion without restarting an old curve at the wrong time.

Urban Dynamics: 100 Shared Tasks

The main study compares three complete navigation systems on 20 X-intersection scenes with five frozen routes per scene: 300 runs, 100 tasks per method. Each task lasts at most 30 s. The compact-quad model integrates at 0.02 s with world updates at 0.1 s. Planner integrations differ, so this is not a pure safety-layer ablation on one identical EGO executable.

Cross-Planner Study: 180 Runs

A separate study evaluates native and TRUCE-integrated EGO, MINCO and SANDO on 30 shared easy MetaUrban development tasks per arm. Fake sim uses an ideal translational double integrator with vehicle limits inactive and a forward virtual depth camera. These outcomes are not pooled with X100 dynamics.

Computation and travel costs

Whole-cycle median latency increases by 5.9× for EGO, 13.8× for MINCO and 1.24× for SANDO, using unrounded values. On common fake-sim arrivals, mean travel time increases by 29.6%, 114.4% and 81.2%, respectively. MINCO and SANDO medians, and all three p99 summaries, exceed the 300 ms decision interval. Synchronous simulation does not expose the vehicle to computation delay.

X100 records solver and selection components, not full Truce-EGO cycle timings, so no equal-scope computation comparison is available for that study. Paired X100 travel increases are 6.10 s versus ROS EGO and 4.07 s versus Gatekeeper.

Limitations

The studies use simulator semantics and configured geometry interfaces; X100 static recovery checks access simulator static geometry. They do not establish detector performance or unknown-map generalization. Easy fake-sim tasks do not establish cross-planner robustness on harder environments. Neither study isolates individual modules through ablation.
